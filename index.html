About Sanatan Dharma

History, Philosophy, and Core Principles

Sanatan Dharma is the eternal way of life rooted in the Vedas, emphasizing Dharma (righteous living), Karma (action), and Moksha (liberation). Its teachings inspire harmony with self, society, and the cosmos.

Vedic Roots

Originating from the Rig, Yajur, Sama, and Atharva Vedas, Sanatan Dharma treasures revelation through mantra, ritual, and philosophical inquiry.

Dharma & Moksha

Dharma guides righteous living, while Moksha offers liberation from the cycle of birth and rebirth through knowledge and devotion.

Living Traditions

Temples, pilgrimages, and sacred festivals keep the tradition vibrant, uniting communities in devotion and service.

The Four Purusharthas

Dharma (Righteousness) Artha (Prosperity) Kama (Fulfillment) Moksha (Liberation)

Deities

Divine Expressions of the Supreme

Each deity represents a sacred aspect of cosmic consciousness, offering guidance, protection, and inspiration.

Brahma

Role: Creator of the universe.

Attributes: Wisdom, knowledge, creative power.

Myth: Emerged from the cosmic lotus.

Temples: Pushkar Brahma Temple, Rajasthan.

Vishnu

Role: Preserver and protector.

Attributes: Compassion, balance, order.

Myth: Avataras restore dharma.

Temples: Tirupati Balaji, Andhra Pradesh.

Shiva

Role: Transformer and liberator.

Attributes: Detachment, compassion, meditation.

Myth: The cosmic dance of Tandava.

Temples: Kashi Vishwanath, Varanasi.

Lakshmi

Role: Goddess of prosperity.

Attributes: Abundance, grace, generosity.

Myth: Emerged during Samudra Manthan.

Temples: Mahalakshmi Temple, Kolhapur.

Saraswati

Role: Goddess of wisdom.

Attributes: Learning, arts, sacred sound.

Myth: Inspiration for music and knowledge.

Temples: Basar Saraswati Temple, Telangana.

Durga

Role: Divine protector.

Attributes: Strength, courage, compassion.

Myth: Triumph of good over evil.

Temples: Vaishno Devi, Jammu.

Sacred Texts and Living Wisdom

From the Vedas to the Bhagavad Gita, the scriptures provide guidance for righteous living, spiritual growth, and self-realization.

Vedas

Overview: The oldest spiritual texts, composed of hymns and rituals.

Key Teachings: Cosmic order (Rta), sacred sound, ritual harmony.

Upanishads

Overview: Philosophical dialogues on the nature of reality and self.

Key Teachings: Atman, Brahman, liberation through wisdom.

Key Teachings: Karma Yoga, Bhakti, equanimity.

Rituals

Daily and Sacred Practices

Rituals align the devotee with divine energy through devotion, gratitude, and sacred action.

Puja

  1. Cleanse the space and set the altar.
  2. Invoke the deity with mantra and offerings.
  3. Offer flowers, incense, and light.

Yajna

  1. Prepare the sacred fire with wood and ghee.
  2. Chant Vedic mantras with offerings.
  3. Conclude with blessings and sharing prasad.

Aarti

  1. Light the lamp and ring the bell.
  2. Circle the lamp before the deity.
  3. Sing devotional hymns together.

Sanskars

  1. Name-giving, initiation, marriage, and last rites.
  2. Honor life stages with sacred vows.
  3. Receive blessings from elders and priests.

Philosophy

Timeless Teachings for Inner Transformation

Sanatan Dharma embraces multiple schools of thought, uniting them through the pursuit of truth and liberation.

Karma & Reincarnation

Every action leaves an imprint, guiding the soul through cycles of birth until liberation is attained.

"As you sow, so shall you reap."

Atman & Brahman

The individual soul (Atman) is ultimately one with the universal consciousness (Brahman).

"The Self is the light within all beings."

Advaita Vedanta

Non-dualism teaches that the ultimate reality is one without a second.

"Truth is one; sages call it by many names." — Rig Veda
