About Sanatan Dharma

Ancient Roots, Living Wisdom

Sanatan Dharma is one of the oldest living spiritual traditions in the world, rooted in the pursuit of truth, compassion, and harmony.

Core Principles

  • Dharma: living with righteousness and balance.
  • Karma: every action has a spiritual consequence.
  • Reincarnation: the soul journeys through cycles of life.
  • Moksha: liberation through wisdom and devotion.

Sacred Symbols

The Om, lotus, and Trishul represent the cosmic sound, purity, and divine balance. These symbols guide seekers toward inner stillness and reverence.

A dialogue on duty, devotion, and self-realization.

Festivals

Celebrations of Light, Joy, and Devotion

Festivals unite communities with devotion, art, and sacred rituals.

Diwali

Festival of Lights celebrating the victory of light over darkness.

Rituals: Lakshmi Puja, lamp lighting, community feasts.

Holi

Festival of Colors honoring love, unity, and the arrival of spring.

Rituals: Color play, bonfires, devotional songs.

Navratri

Nine nights of devotion honoring the Divine Mother.

Rituals: Garba, fasting, Durga Puja.

Ganesh Chaturthi

Celebration of Ganesha with joyous processions and offerings.

Rituals: Modak offerings, immersion ceremonies.

Durga Puja

Commemorates the triumph of divine courage over adversity.

Rituals: Pandals, cultural performances, prayers.

Raksha Bandhan

Honors the sacred bond of protection and care between siblings.

Rituals: Rakhi tying, blessings, sharing sweets.

Philosophy

Pathways to Truth and Liberation

The philosophy of Sanatan Dharma embraces inquiry, devotion, and ethical living.

Four Yogas

Bhakti (devotion), Karma (action), Jnana (wisdom), and Raja (meditation).

Ahimsa & Compassion

Non-violence and kindness to all beings are central to spiritual life.

Unity of the Self

The Atman (self) is ultimately one with Brahman (the absolute).

Cosmic Order

Rta and Dharma maintain balance in the universe and society.

Rituals and Practices

Guided Paths for Daily Worship

Rituals connect the devotee to the divine through intention, mantra, and offering.

Puja

  1. Prepare a clean altar with flowers and a lamp.
  2. Invoke the deity with a mantra.
  3. Offer incense, water, and prasad.
  4. Close with a moment of gratitude.

Yajna

  1. Arrange the sacred fire with guidance.
  2. Offer ghee and herbs while chanting.
  3. Seek blessings for harmony and prosperity.

Aarti

  1. Light the diya and ring the bell.
  2. Circle the flame clockwise before the deity.
  3. Sing devotional hymns in unity.

Samskaras

  1. Celebrate rites like naming, marriage, and initiation.
  2. Honor elders and ancestors with gratitude.
  3. Mark life transitions with sacred vows.
